# Paper 0: From Intelligence to Autonomous Execution

## Working thesis

AI is turning execution from an internal organizational capability into a programmable economic resource. Existing systems can identify agents, constrain wallets, and settle payments, but they do not yet provide a common framework for comparing the value and responsibility created by autonomous actions.

## Abstract

This paper defines autonomous execution as an authorized transformation of external state under uncertainty in pursuit of an objective. It argues that execution is distinct from information production and computation because it consumes delegated authority, creates consequences, and requires accountability. Drawing on transaction-cost economics, organizational theory, platform economics, and digital market design, the paper asks when autonomous execution should be coordinated through hierarchy, deterministic workflow, centralized routing, platforms, or open markets. It does not propose a protocol. It establishes a research agenda for task representation, authority, evidence, contribution evaluation, responsibility, and settlement.

## Intended contributions

1. Define autonomous execution as a distinct object of study.
2. Identify the gap between existing economic and technical frameworks and agents acting under delegated authority.
3. Establish falsifiable conditions for when execution may become market-coordinated.

## Outline

1. From intelligence to execution
2. Economic coordination and transaction costs
3. Definition and properties of autonomous execution
4. Why current models are incomplete
5. Lessons from firms, platforms, and digital markets
6. Coordination choices: hierarchy, workflow, routing, platform, or market
7. Financial agents as an initial experimental domain
8. Research questions and falsifiable hypotheses
9. Limits and conclusion

## Boundaries

Paper 0 will not specify tokens, smart contracts, wallet architecture, receipt schemas, or a marketplace implementation. Those mechanisms require separate evidence and design work.
